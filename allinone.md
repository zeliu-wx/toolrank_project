## Summary of all searched results

### 1. Overview

The retained corpus contains 31 screened tool/paper relationships covering six
tools from 2019–2026. Exact timing evidence is much scarcer than tool mentions:
only homogeneous, end-to-end paper means were admitted to arithmetic summaries.

### 2. Trends

Recent comparison studies increasingly report fixed campaigns, throughput,
time-to-bug, or completion rates instead of end-to-end mean completion time.
Compiler incompatibility is a recurring cause of failure for older symbolic and
fuzzing tools. LLM/ML detector papers frequently report accuracy and F1 while
omitting inference-time denominators.

### 3. Key themes

1. **Comparable per-contract means:** HoneyBadger and SAILFISH have several
   independent paper-level means, but hardware and datasets remain heterogeneous.
2. **Size-normalized runtime:** GPTScan is defensibly comparable only in
   seconds/KLoC; project and case values cannot be relabeled as contracts.
3. **Budget-bounded fuzzing:** SMARTIAN studies primarily report 30/60-minute
   campaigns, throughput, and time-to-bug, not completion-time means.
4. **Missing ML latency:** MANDO-HGT comparisons publish effectiveness and
   completion counts, but no exact end-to-end inference duration.
5. **Provenance hazards:** copied baseline tables, plot readings, cumulative
   totals, and mixed denominators can create precise-looking but invalid means.

### 7. Recommendations for reading

1. **The Art of The Scam (2019):** clearest HoneyBadger denominator, timeout,
   completion count, and mean-runtime evidence.
2. **SAILFISH (2022):** foundational tool evaluation with a large real-world
   corpus and explicit per-contract mean.
3. **SMARTIAN (2021):** essential for understanding why campaign budget is not
   the same quantity as analysis completion time.
4. **GPTScan (2024):** gives a replayable total and seconds/KLoC normalization.
5. **VulHunter (2023):** the only verified exact VulHunter per-contract runtime
   located in this bounded search.
